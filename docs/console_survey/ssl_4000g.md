## SSL 4000G: survey report (label: ssl)

### 1. Header
- **Document:** `SSL-4000G/SSL-4000G-Console-Service-Manual-v1_1.pdf`, 335 pages, © 1993, drawings dated 1980-1995. The text layer is OCR, good enough for the connector tables and descriptions. The schematics are 300 dpi scans, which I read by cropping.
- **Pages read (PDF page numbers):**
  - p3: console block schematic, 4000G+.
  - p7: 611G front-panel parts with pot values.
  - p9-13: FET function table and bus edge-connector pinouts.
  - p14: 611 fusing.
  - p15: routing pan.
  - p16: small fader / solo.
  - p21: routing switch card 82E07.
  - p26: 82E291 channel amps, E4 version (line input, line gain, Ø, insert return, pan buffers, input select).
  - p31: 82E292 filters / EQ.
  - p37: 82E13 VCA / AFL / overload.
  - p39: 82E294 group amps (multitrack mix amp and balanced group output).
  - p41-44: 82E293 logic text.
  - p105, 122-123, 157: frame wiring, ribbon and power-connector pinouts.
  - p176: 651G master block.
  - p190: 82E22 monitor outputs.
  - p200: 82E25 main quad outputs.
  - p203: 82E26 quad-bus mix amp.
  - p242-245: PSU specification and rail scheme.
  - p250: 82E98 regulator.
  - p257: 82E300 PSU delay unit.
  - p270-280: bargraph meter description.
- **Missing:**
  - **Specifications:** no console performance page (no noise, CMRR, crosstalk, frequency response or headroom figures). The only "specification" pages are the PSU's and a stray Eagle microphone sheet (p181).
  - **Sends and insert send:** the cue / aux send circuits and the insert-send driver are not drawn. They sit on the 611 motherboard, which the manual does not show.
  - **Fader:** the VCA fader card 82E17 itself was not read.

### 2. Published specs
- **Operating level:** +4 dBm = 0 VU (bargraph alignment, p269 / p280).
- **Rails:**
  - **Audio:** the PSU's ±20 V outputs are set to **±18.5 V** at the master power bus. They are 25 A each, with over-voltage trips at ±22.5 V and 5 A short-circuit current (p244-245).
  - **Logic:** 6.5 V, 7 A, stacked on +18.5 V, so logic sits between +12 and +18.5 V relative to 0 V.
  - **Other:** 48 V at 0.5 A, plus 250-263 V for the plasma bargraphs.
  - **Current draw:** a 40-channel console takes about 20 A on the audio rails and 2-6 A on logic (p245).
  - **Grounding:** "0V = analogue common and chassis" (p245).
- **Headroom (inferred):** 5534s on ±18.5 V clip at about +21-22 dBu. The channel overload LED is marked "+20 dBu" (p3).
- **Noise, CMRR, crosstalk, frequency response:** not in this document.

### 3. Topics

**T1 Line input** (read, p26, 82E291 E4)
- AC-coupled in: C15 / C16 2.2 µF (marked ".1%"), then R28 / R29 16K9 0.1 % into a 5534 (IC5).
- IC5's feedback is 10K7 0.1 % with 22 pF. Its + leg returns, through R31 10K7 0.1 %, to the output of IC6, a 5534 that inverts IC5 (5K11 / 5K11). IC5 and IC6 are therefore a fully differential pair.
- **My calculation:** each leg sits at about −10 dB for the differential input, and the IC5 / IC6 balanced pair carries about −4 dB. That leaves room for roughly +28 dBu at the input.
- DC servo: LF351 (IC14), 1M / 100 nF / 330K, back into IC5's − input. R80 1M bleeds the − leg.
- No buffer and no CMRR trim on the line input (the mic amp has one).
- **My calculation:** each leg is about 17K into a virtual node. A 1K source imbalance gives roughly 25-30 dB CMRR.
- **vs ours:** different. Ours buffers each leg (1M), so it keeps 54 dB CMRR against a 1K source. SSL trades CMRR for an attenuating input with a lot of headroom. Ours is better for synths with unbalanced outputs.

**T2 Line gain** (read, p26 / p7)
- ±20 dB from a dual 10K lin pot with a centre detent ("CT/IND", p7).
- The two pot sections are fed from the IC5 / IC6 balanced pair through 1K1 each.
- One wiper drives IC7's − input. That section's far end is IC7's own output through R36 1K1, so it is the feedback.
- The other wiper drives IC7's + input. That section's far end goes to ground through R37 1K1.
- IC7 is a 5534 differential stage. A 0 dB trim sits on IC7's − input: VR2 50K + R38 36K from IC5's output.
- **vs ours:** same idea (a separate stage, detent at 0 dB). SSL makes it differential across a dual pot and spans ±20 dB against our ±15 dB.

**T3 Polarity** (read, p26)
- Placed after the mic / line FET selector.
- IC8 is a 5534 unity differential amp: 10K0 resistors, 100 pF across the feedback, both inputs coupled by 100 µF.
- The Ø key chooses which input carries the signal; the other is grounded.
- **vs ours:** different place. We swap the legs ahead of the receiver; SSL switches + / − on an extra stage. Ours costs no stage.

**T4 HPF** (read, p31)
- 18 dB/oct: a first-order section (C15 220 nF, follower IC11) followed by a unity Sallen-Key (C17 / C18 220 nF, IC12).
- Tuned as rheostats by a 47K A/log triple pot (1K8 end-stops) with an in/out switch.
- **My calculation:** 1K8-48.8K against 220 nF gives about 15-400 Hz (panel 20-350 Hz).
- A 12 dB/oct LPF follows (10K lin dual, 6n8 / 3n3).
- **vs ours:** ours is 12 dB/oct KHN tuned by dividers. SSL is 18 dB/oct tuned by rheostats, as eq_survey.md found.

**T5 EQ**: skipped as instructed. The EQ (with filters) can be switched to the channel, the monitor path or the dynamics side-chain (82E312, p22). That routing is irrelevant for us.

**T6 Insert** (read for the return, p26; the send is not drawn)
- The return is **balanced**:
  - IC9 is a 5534 differential amp, 30K1 0.1 % ×4, with 2.2 µF on both legs, R48 20K across the legs and 22 pF on both sides.
  - The output goes through 100 µF, 10R and a 47K bleed.
- The send appears only as "insert send ± … on via R25 / Insert PRE switch" (p12). The circuit is not shown.
- The block diagram shows PRE (pre-EQ) and IN keys.
- **vs ours:** ours is an unbalanced follower on a single TRS jack. SSL uses separate balanced send / return on a patchbay.

**T7 Mute** (read, p26 / p37 / p190)
- **Channel input cut:** a J175 in series plus a J112 shunt (RC48 / RC4A), gates through 10M.
- **Large-fader cut:** a BC212 drives the VCA control voltage (DC mute in the dbx 202X).
- **AFL switch:**
  - J112 in series with its gate bootstrapped to the source through 10M, plus a J175 shunt.
  - The gate is slowed by C10 100 nF and 10M.
- **Monitor outputs:** a J112 series / J175 shunt mute driven from a 4071 through 5K6 / 10 nF.
- **vs ours:** the same JFET practice (ours is J112 shunts after 4K7 with a 20 ms ramp). SSL mutes the main path in the VCA.

**T8 Fader** (read, p7 / p26 / p37)
- The large fader is a **VCA fader**: DC control into a dbx 202X, with a 5534 current-to-voltage stage (T3), under automation / Total Recall.
- The small fader is a passive 65 mm log track. IC10, a 5534 follower, selects which fader feeds the routing (J112s through 10M, 4K3 / 100K bleeds).
- No post-amp.
- **vs ours:** not comparable. Ours is a passive fader with a +10 dB post-amp; the VCA only exists for automation.

**T9 Pan** (read, p15 / p26)
- Dual 10K lin. A 6K81 resistor from each section's top to its wiper shapes the law.
- **My calculation:** about −4 dB at centre (5K against 5K ∥ 6K81).
- The PAN key bypasses the pot (both sides unpanned), as ours does.
- Each side is buffered: 10 µF, a 22K / 22K divider, then a 5534 at ×2 (2K2 / 2K2), so net unity. Then 100 µF, 10R and a 47K bleed.
- Link LK2 can lift the divider's foot off 0 V.
- **vs ours:** same buffering. The law differs: −4 dB against our −6 dB.

**T10 Aux sends** (panel p7 / p59; block p3)
- Stereo cue (level + pan) and cues 1-4, each with a PRE key and a SMALL FADER key (which fader feeds the send).
- Pots: 10K log (p7). The buses are fed from **15K resistor packs** (p13).
- The circuit itself is not drawn.
- **vs ours:** similar function. SSL's bus resistor is 15K against our 22K.

**T11 Routing** (read, p21; resistor note p10 / p13)
- One mechanical key per bus. The common output goes to the bus's 15K pack. When off, the key connects that output to **0 V**; when on, it connects the odd or even pan output.
- So the resistor's **source end** is grounded when unrouted; the bus noise gain stays constant (**inferred** from the pack note).
- A second pole lights the LED (300R, between +20 V and +13 V logic).
- **vs ours:** same principle (the bus load never changes) at the other end of the resistor. Mechanical contacts have no on-resistance distortion, so source-end switching is harmless there. For our DG413, the bus end was right.

**T12 Mix-bus summing** (read, p39 / p203)
- **Multitrack bus amp** (one per I/O module):
  - LT1028, inverting, with the bus coupled into the virtual earth through C1 **2200 µF 6V3**.
  - Feedback 432R + a 22K log group trim (−30…0 dB) ∥ 22K, with 100 pF.
  - Then a unity 5534 re-inverter (10K / 10K), 220 µF and 10R.
- **Quad (stereo) mix amp:**
  - The bus enters through 2 × 470 µF back to back. Their midpoint is polarised to about +1.6 V by 1M / 100K from +18 V. Then L1 220 µH.
  - D8 / D9 antiparallel clamp it to "mix bus 0V".
  - A discrete front end: two paralleled LM394 matched pairs, 22 µH / 30R emitter networks, a BC184 tail, and 2K43 loads into an NE5534.
  - Feedback 15K + 1K.
  - Then a unity NE5534 re-inverter (15K / 15K, 10 µF in) and 47R.
- **vs ours:** same structure (summer then re-inverter). SSL uses a 15K bus against our 4K7, with a very low-noise discrete / LT1028 front end. It blocks DC at the summing node, so the amp's DC gain is unity; we AC-couple at every source.

**T13 Bus ground** (read p203 / p39; meaning inferred)
- **Quad amp:** the non-inverting input (the LM394 "a" bases) is the "MIX BUS 0V" line. That line is tied to local 0 V through R62 1K + R1 20K trim, with C21 1 nF.
- **Multitrack amp:** + input to connector pin 3 through R1 1K, with RV2 50K "CMRR TRIM" to 0 V.
- **Ribbons and edge connectors:** 0 V runs between **every** bus line (S5E-S8E, p122-123; edge pins p10-13). Each module also has its own "Mix 0V" pin (p11 pin 78).
- **vs ours:** the same idea as our GCANCEL: a sensed bus ground into the summer's + input. SSL ties it to master ground through 1-21K with a trim. Ours has no tie (only a 100K keeper) and no trim.

**T14 Bus AC coupling** (read, p26 / p39 / p203)
- 100 µF 10 V polar + 47K at the pan buffers and patch return.
- 2200 µF at the multitrack summing node; 2 × 470 µF biased back-to-back at the quad bus.
- **vs ours:** SSL couples at both the source and the summing node, with polar electrolytics. Ours uses NP / film.

**T15 PFL / solo** (read p16 / p37; text p41-44)
- **Destructive solo-in-place:** any solo drives a "VCA / small-fader solo cut bus" (DC logic), which cuts every channel not soloed. Each channel has a "solo isolate" (pull switch).
- **Master "AFL status":** raises a "solo disable bus", and solos go non-destructively to the AFL bus instead.
- **AFL tap:** a link selects PFL or AFL. The tap goes through 10 µF onto the AFL mix bus via the J112 / J175 switch.
- **vs ours:** ours is PFL only, with a DC detect line. SSL adds SIP plus AFL, at the cost of one logic bus and per-channel cut logic.

**T16 Direct out** (read, p39)
- "Direct" sends the channel to its own group output stage, bypassing the multitrack bus (IC2's output to the DIRECT switch).
- That output is balanced (see T17's output stage).
- **vs ours:** ours is a separate impedance-balanced TRS from aux 6. SSL reuses the group output.

**T17 Master outputs** (read, p39 / p200 / p190)
- **Group outputs:** a cross-coupled 5534 balanced pair: 39K2 / 30K1 / 301K 0.1 %, 75R build-outs, 220 µF, and a RV1 1K balance trim.
- **Main quad outputs:**
  - An SSL "85502" hybrid driver: inverting input 16K9 with 13K4 feedback and 33 pF.
  - 220 µF on each leg, then 1K5 + VR1 5K trim to 0 V, with a BAL / UNBAL link.
  - A 5534 diff amp (30K1 / 19K1) feeds the monitor and SLS. Slate is injected by J112 / J175.
  - **No relays and no power-on mute** on the mains. Monitors mute by FET.
- **vs ours:** ours uses DRV135 + a G6K relay with power-on delay. Ours is safer; SSL relies on the PSU.

**T18 Meters** (read p37; text p270-271)
- **Channel overload:**
  - Three overload inputs (34A / 36B / 38C, from different stages), **both polarities** (1N4148 ×6).
  - Two LF347 comparators against about ±10 V. **My calculation:** that is about +19-20 dBu, matching "+20 dBu" on p3.
  - The comparators OR into a hold (100 nF; 1M to −18 V) and an LF347 LED driver.
- **Channel meters:** VU / PPM moving coil, or plasma bargraphs with VU (300 ms, 1.5 % overshoot) and PPM (3.3 µF, 2.8 s decay) ballistics.
- **vs ours:** our OL detector reads one point (PRESRC), positive half only (one diode). SSL watches three stages full-wave.

**T19 Power** (read p14 / p157 / p242-250)
- **PSU:** linear: 100,000 µF 40 V reservoir, 5 × 2N5886 + 2N3055 pass, LF351 error amp, current limit, BTY91 SCR crowbar, thermal breakers, a 1 Ω inrush resistor shorted by a delayed relay.
- **Distribution:** per 8 modules, a 19-way BICC connector with 3 × 15 A on each rail, 2 × 15 A on 0 V and chassis separate; 6 A breakers per group of 8.
- **Per module:** 2 A fuses per rail, 24 V zeners from each rail to 0 V, 10R to the front panel.
- **Per card:** 10R + 22 µF on each rail. The VCA card has its own 78L15 / 79L15.
- **Logic:** the 6.5 V supply stacked on +18.5 V keeps LED and logic currents out of 0 V.
- **vs ours:** the same crowbar / linear philosophy, and lower rails (±18.5 against ±17). Our separate GNDD with one tie (NT1) does the job SSL's stacked logic rail does.

**T20 Op-amps / stability** (read)
- NE5534 / 5534AN throughout, 22 pF compensation (pins 5-8) and 22 pF across feedback.
- LF351 servos and FET drivers, LT1028 bus amps, LM394 discrete front end, LF347 / CA324 logic and VCA control.
- 10R build-outs and 100-220 µF output caps, 22 / 220 µH inductors in the mix amp.
- **vs ours:** the same practice (C0G caps, build-outs). Ours uses newer parts.

**T21 Levels**: +4 dBm nominal against our 0 dBu internal; about +21 dBu clip (inferred). No noise figures to compare.

**T22 Construction** (read)
- One module per channel: a 611 motherboard with plug-in cards on edge connectors.
- Bus cards, one per 8 modules, carry 88 + 77 bus pins with grounds between the bus lines.
- 20-way ribbons with alternate 0 V run from the bus cards to the master.
- **vs ours:** our 34-way ribbon is the Delta-style approach, at a much smaller scale.

**Not covered by these documents:** T5 detail (skipped), the cue / aux send circuit, the insert-send driver, the PFL detect / auto-switch hardware (SSL uses logic status buses instead), and noise / CMRR / crosstalk figures.

### 4. Worth considering for goa-console
1. **Overload detection, full-wave and at more than one point.**
   - *Why:* synth saws and pulses are asymmetric and can clip on the negative half first. A boost inside the EQ or HPF can also clip an internal node while PRESRC still looks fine.
   - *Cost:* a second 1N4148 into the OL hold node from an inverted copy, or a negative-side comparator. That is an LM393 half, about 4 resistors and 2 diodes, plus a tap at the KHN / EQ output.
   - *Fit:* good; low cost.
2. **Solo-in-place, using a spare bus pin** (1 / 3 / 5 / 7 / 19).
   - *How:* a DC "solo cut" line; every strip not soloed engages its existing J112 mute; returns isolated.
   - *Why:* when mixing synths you want to hear a part post-fader, panned, with its returns. PFL is mono and pre-fader.
   - *Cost:* one comparator per strip, a solo-isolate jumper, and master logic. It is destructive, so it needs a clear master SIP / PFL mode.
   - *Fit:* plausible. Skip it if PFL is enough on stage.
3. **A softer pan law.**
   - SSL is about −4 dB at centre (6K81 law resistors on a 10K lin dual). Ours is −6 dB, so a centred mono synth sits 6 dB below hard-panned.
   - *Cost:* two resistors.
   - *Caveat:* it changes the levels that sim_console checks and the panel "0" marks. This is a taste decision.
4. **Bus-ground tie / trim.**
   - SSL references its summers to a sensed bus 0 V with a 1-21K trimmed tie. Ours deliberately has none (a 22 Ω tie cost about 45 dB in our sim).
   - A trim cannot cancel residue that is spread along the ribbon. **Not recommended**; listed only as a difference.
5. **Balanced insert return.**
   - SSL's return is a 0.1 % difference amp. Ours is an unbalanced follower on one TRS insert jack.
   - It only helps with separate send / return jacks or a patchbay, which a synth console with TRS inserts doesn't have. **Not recommended** unless the insert moves to separate jacks.

**Where ours is weaker than every reasonable practice:** nothing found. Ours is better in places:
- Our input CMRR against unbalanced synths is better than SSL's unbuffered 17K legs.
- We have output relays with power-on delay; SSL has none on the mains.

Ground lines between bus conductors on the ribbon are SSL practice, but our measured crosstalk (−94 dB at 20 kHz) doesn't call for them.
