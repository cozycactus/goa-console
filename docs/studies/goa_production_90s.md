# How goa trance was produced in the 1990s: the work at the console

Study, 2026-11-11. No design change: observations only (last section). Question from Ruslan: how goa trance tracks
were made in 1993-1999, how the work at the mixer went, and whether the Mackie 32•8 really was the desk of the goa
collectives.

**Method.** Only sources that were opened and read on 2026-11-11 are used (a second pass the same day, through
Ruslan's Chrome and archive.org, added S53-S56 and the Discogs, video and Muzik findings); each is listed in "Sources"
with title, publication, date, link and a strength note. Every statement carries a mark and a source number:

- **[D]** documented: the source says it.
- **[I]** inferred: my conclusion, with what it is drawn from.
- **[N]** not found: searched for, no source.

Strength: *strong* = the artist's or engineer's own words in an interview or on their own site, or a magazine studio
feature / review; *medium* = first-hand but a forum post, release credits or a label statement; *weak* = second-hand
or anonymous. "Retrospective" means said years later, which matters for dates and details. The sources' wording is
paraphrased throughout (one short quotation). How close a source is to goa trance is said where it is not a goa act:
"same scene" = the UK psychedelic / Planet Dog / Butterfly circle; "neighbour" = other dance music of the same years.

## 1. What is established

1. **The Mackie 32•8: partly confirmed.** It is documented for the central goa producer and for two acts of the same
   UK scene, and for nobody else:
   - Simon Posford (Hallucinogen): "all of Posford's output has been recorded on a Mackie 32:8 analogue desk", from
     the first Hallucinogen album to the 2007 one; he says he had one since the model came out **[D, S2]**. The
     album's year, 1995, is from S15. The article's photo caption says only Mackie 8-bus, without the size.
   - Eat Static: a Mackie 32:8:2 with the 24-channel expander, in the gear list of an article published in January
     1997 **[D, S1]**.
   - Banco de Gaia: a Mackie 32:8:2 in 1997 (a Studiomaster Proline 16:4 in 1994) **[D, S10, S9]**.
   - The sleeve credits place "Twisted" at Posford's own Hallucinogen Sound Laboratories, London, and "The Lone
     Deranger" at Hallucinogen Sound Labs, Dorset; The Infinity Project's TIP album "Mystical Experiences" is credited
     as recorded at Hallucinogen Studio, Battersea, with Posford as engineer **[D, S54, medium]**. With S2, those
     three albums went through his Mackie **[I]**.
   - No source names a Mackie 8•Bus for any continental or Israeli goa act, nor for a Flying Rhino or Blue Room act
     **[N]**. Named counter-examples: Juno Reactor's Raindirk Symphony (1997) **[D, S4]**, the DDA AMR24 at
     Youth's Butterfly Studios (1992) **[D, S3]**, a Soundcraft at MFG (2000) **[D, S39]**.
   - So "often used in goa collectives" stands on three documented studios, one of them that of the producer whom
     Youth credits with defining the genre **[D, S14]**, plus unread forum claims **[I, from S1, S2, S10 and the
     not-founds above]**. It is not contradicted, and it is not shown to be the rule.
2. **A sequencer played the synths and samplers live through the desk.** Atari with Cubase, Notator or Creator
   **[D, S1, S3, S8, S9, S41]**, Mac or PC from about 1996 **[D, S1, S4, S10]**.
3. **Mixing live to DAT with no multitrack is documented for two goa acts**, Juno Reactor in 1995 and Man With No
   Name in 1994-96 **[D, S5, S6]**, and for neighbours (Orbital, Underworld, Garnier) **[D, S25, S26, S28]**. For
   Eat Static and OOOD it is inferred: the first from a gear list with a DAT machine and the writer's remark that
   the studio had no multitrack facilities (the same list has an 8-track), the second from a MIDI-only studio that
   played its finished tracks from DAT **[I, from S1, S16]**. That it was the typical method is my reading of these
   few cases **[I]**; Juno Reactor in 1997 and Butterfly in 1992 worked to 24-track tape **[D, S4, S3]**.
4. **The mix was a hands-on performance.** Fader rides, buttons, effects thrown in by hand during long takes
   (Juno Reactor 1995) **[D, S5]**; automation was declined by choice (Youth, Toby Marks, Ed Wynne) **[D, S3, S10,
   S34]** or partial (Juno Reactor 1997) **[D, S4]**. Posford shows the same on video at his 32-channel desk
   (uploaded 2008): channels switched in and out with their on / off buttons, sends to echoes and reverbs, the echo
   sent back into itself, a boosted EQ band swept by hand **[D, S53]**.
5. **No recall, no parts kept.** Man With No Name could not supply parts for remixes of his 1994-96 singles: he had
   mixed to DAT and kept no back-up **[D, S6]**. Toby Marks: a board set up again never sounds quite the same
   **[D, S10]**.
6. **The in-line desk was sold for its inputs.** Reviews of 1993-95 present the second path of each channel (Mackie
   "Mix-B") as an extra line input at mixdown **[D, S20, S21]**, one of them naming sequenced instruments
   **[D, S22]**. No goa artist was found describing how they laid out those paths **[N]**.
7. **The decade's change:** ADAT added beside the live pass by the neighbours and System 7 (1993-95) **[D, S25, S24,
   S8]**; audio in the computer beside the sequencer (1996-98) **[D, S1, S10, S42]**; newcomers starting in software
   (a tracker, Cubase on a PC) by 1999-2001 **[D, S40, S52]**; Posford still mixing on the analogue desk in 2007
   **[D, S2]**.
8. **Not established:** the console of most named goa acts; any goa artist's own account of aux and effects
   routing, high-pass habits, subgroup use, running the desk hot, or solo / PFL use (section 5).

## 3. How the console was worked

### 3.1 Inputs

- The Mackie 8•Bus came in 16, 24 and 32 channels (£4395 for the 32:8:2 in December 1993), with a 24-channel expander
  announced at launch. Each channel has a second path, Mix-B, which the review presents as a tape monitor while
  recording and an extra input at mixdown; six stereo aux returns were counted as generous for the price **[D, S20]**.
- Other reviews say the same of the class: more inputs is what every buyer asks for, an in-line desk doubles its line
  inputs at mixdown, a 32-channel Spirit Studio LC mixes 82 inputs **[D, S21]**; the Behringer Eurodesk's second path is
  described as an extra line input for sequenced instruments **[D, S22]**; the small Allen & Heath GS1 follows the same
  convention **[D, S49]**.
- The limits were known and printed: the second path has no aux sends of its own (on the Mackie it borrows two of the
  channel's) **[D, S20, S22]** and little or no EQ, so it ends up with sources that shape their own sound: synths,
  samplers, drum machines **[D, S23]**.
- What the artists say: Eat Static ran the 32-channel Mackie plus the 24-channel expander, and bought a second Akai
  sampler for mixing because the ten outputs of one were too few for their layered tracks (twenty with two)
  **[D, S1]**. 32 + 24 = 56 channel strips, 112 inputs counting Mix-B **[I, arithmetic from S1 and S20]**. Benji
  Vaughan (Prometheus) describes his desk as 32 outputs and outboard patched in, all to be pulled out before
  anything else can be worked on **[D, S2]**.
- Other ways to more inputs, documented near the scene: a keyboard sub-mixer feeding the main desk (System 7)
  **[D, S8]**; a second desk on stage (Orbital, Underworld) **[D, S25, S27]**.
- Posford, walking up one channel of his desk on video, passes over a set of level controls above the pan as being
  for more things coming in **[D, S53]**. That these are the Mix-B controls, in use as extra inputs, is my reading:
  the desk is not named in the video, S2 names it **[I]**.
- How a goa act laid out its Mix-B paths in the 1990s **[N]**.

### 3.2 Aux sends and effects

- The Mackie has six aux buses but four send knobs per channel (the second pair switches between buses 3-4 and 5-6),
  and Mix-B takes two of them when it needs sends **[D, S20]**. A Spirit Studio LC reaches ten sends by using its
  monitor path as a stereo send; the Ghost has ten aux buses **[D, S21, S31]**. A reviewer of 1994: one can never
  have enough effect sends **[D, S23]**.
- Posford in 2007, of one desk he likes (the SSL AWS900): perhaps not enough physical aux sends, and only a
  24-channel version **[D, S2]**. One reservation about one desk; the article says no more on sends.
- Sends were ridden by hand during the pass. Juno Reactor: reverbs and delays thrown in in real time (1995)
  **[D, S5]**. Banco de Gaia rejects an assignable digital desk because a reverb send has to be hit on one snare
  beat while other things are being adjusted **[D, S10, same scene]**.
- Eat Static's gear list has thirteen processors **[D, S1]**; sorted by me from the model names: two reverbs, one
  delay, four multi-effects, two compressors, noise gates, a noise-reduction unit and two enhancers **[I]**. Prana had
  no compressors at all: synths, a few effects and the desk **[D, S7]**.
  Underworld's 1996 album used two multi-effects and one mono delay line kept for feedback and dub delays, and no
  compressors or gates **[D, S26, neighbour]**.
- Posford's demonstration (video uploaded 2008): the send controls feed a rack of echoes and reverbs; a sound is
  sent to an echo and the echo is then sent back to itself; in his live sets the channels are switched in and out,
  sent to the echo, the echo fed back, sent on to other effects, levels ridden **[D, S53]**. An echo that can be
  sent to itself has its return on a path with a send, so on a channel and not on a plain return **[I]**. His
  first effect unit was an Alesis Quadraverb; the rack shown also has Eventide units for reverbs and Distressor
  compressors **[D, S53]**.
- Tempo-synced delay settings, and how many sends a goa mix of the 1990s used **[N]**.

### 3.3 EQ and high-pass

- The Mackie's EQ: shelving high and low (12 kHz, 80 Hz), a fully parametric high-mid (500 Hz-18 kHz), a swept
  low-mid (45 Hz-3 kHz), a 75 Hz low-cut switch, and an EQ that can be split to the Mix-B path **[D, S20]**.
- Posford in 2007 still equalised on the Mackie after equalising in software, and values reaching a knob faster than
  a mouse **[D, S2]**. Ben Watkins credits the Raindirk's EQ, and what it did when the low end was pushed, for the
  sound of "Bible of Dreams" **[D, S4]**.
- Posford on video: the bass sound on the channel he demonstrates has had much of its low end taken off at the
  desk; and a swept band is used as an effect, boosting a fairly low frequency and sweeping it, which he calls a
  sound heard on many trance tracks **[D, S53]**.
- High-pass habits of goa producers beyond that **[N]**. One anonymous forum post claims such project desks had no
  high-pass on line inputs **[D, S46, weak]**; the 8•Bus drawings were not checked for whether its low-cut is in the
  line path, so this is left open.

### 3.4 Subgroups

- The 8•Bus groups can be sent to the mix in mono or as odd / even stereo pairs, and have inserts **[D, S20]**.
- Use of subgroups or bus compression by a goa act **[N]**. Against a habit of drum-bus compression stand Prana's and
  Underworld's statements that they owned no compressors **[D, S7, S26]**.

### 3.5 Gain staging

- A documented habit of running the desk hot in goa **[N]**.
- Outside goa: a 2021 tutorial states that 1990s techno distortion came from the mixer **[D, S50, weak: secondary]**;
  the Prodigy's engineer says he drove everything hard, speaking of the commercial-studio mixes **[D, S29]**;
  anonymous forum posts say the same of drum'n'bass **[D, S30, weak]**.

### 3.6 Solo, PFL and monitoring

- The Mackie's factory solo is post-fader; the manual documents an internal per-channel modification that turns it
  into a mono pre-fade listen **[D, S20]**. Read from the service drawings, it is a stereo, post-pan solo on its own
  pair of buses that does not disturb the mix (`docs/console_survey/mackie.md`, T15). The Ghost offers a switch
  between PFL and a destructive solo-in-place **[D, S31]**.
- Youth (1992) plays the solo buttons during the mix to change the arrangement, complains that no automation records
  them, and judges desks by whether a hand can sweep across the solo row; his own DDA AMR24 passes, the SSLs do not
  **[D, S3]**. That this means a solo that cuts the other channels is my reading: the article does not name the solo
  type **[I]**.
- How a goa producer on a Mackie used solo or PFL **[N]**.
- Monitoring: Eat Static's gear list has Alesis Monitor Two mains and Yamaha NS10 nearfields **[D, S1]**; Butterfly had
  Genelecs and NS10s **[D, S3]**.

### 3.7 Mutes and automation

- The 8•Bus as delivered is entirely manual **[D, S20]**. Mackie's Ultramix (1996) is an external VCA unit for levels
  and mutes, not moving faders **[D, S32]**. The Ghost (1996) has four mute groups and 128 mute snapshots, and full
  mute automation only from an external MIDI sequencer **[D, S31]**. No artist was found describing the use of any
  of these **[N]**.
- The channel on / off buttons as the arrangement tool: Posford brings parts in and out with them, in the studio
  demonstration and in his live sets **[D, S53]**.
- Automation was declined on purpose: Youth had none fitted to the DDA and preferred the harder manual mix **[D,
  S3]**; Ed Wynne of Ozric Tentacles (Eat Static's parent band) treats a manual mix as a take, and finds a good take
  with something wild in it sometimes the more interesting **[D, S34]**; Toby Marks keeps the Mackie and stays away
  from automated mixing **[D, S10]**; Juno Reactor had some automation in 1997 and still mixed with all hands on the
  desk **[D, S4]**.
- The one use of MIDI mutes found: Toby Marks calls the Studiomaster's MIDI muting handy with many hissing analogue
  synths connected **[D, S9]**. When in the track he muted them is not said.

### 3.8 Mixdown to DAT, and editing

- Juno Reactor, 1995: everything recorded live to DAT in long takes **[D, S5]**. Man With No Name: mixed down, onto
  DAT, on to the next record **[D, S6]**.
- OOOD, 1994-2000: on stage, the whole studio, with Cubase playing synths and samplers, alternating with finished
  tracks from a DAT player, until they began to work with multitrack audio **[D, S16]**. That the studio mixes
  went to that DAT is the obvious reading, not a statement **[I]**.
- Eat Static: the article lists a DAT machine and an 8-track and does not say what the mixes were recorded to; the
  writer remarks on the lack of multitrack facilities, and Merv Pepler keeps DAT recordings of nearly every gig
  **[D, S1]**. Mixdown to DAT is inferred from that **[I]**.
- The neighbours describe the same method in more detail. Orbital: straight to two tracks is the first choice; the
  ADAT is used to put synth tweaking down first so that hands are free for the pass **[D, S25]**. Underworld: mixes
  performed live through the console to a portable DAT, a hard-disk recorder hired only to assemble the album
  **[D, S26]**. Laurent Garnier: a loop of all the instruments, mixed live straight to DAT **[D, S28]**.
- Editing several passes together is documented for Youth, though not for a named goa record: many different mixes
  to DAT, then into Sound Tools and cut into one, on tape before that **[D, S11]**; his building had a Sound Tools
  suite in 1992 **[D, S3]**. Youth names Posford as the studio engineer of those years, and the label manager ties
  "Twisted" to Posford's work in the Butterfly studio **[D, S14, S15]**; that the method was used on a goa record
  there does not follow, only that the means were in the house **[I]**. A goa artist saying a track was mixed in
  sections and edited **[N]**.
- DAT was also how the music travelled. In a report from Goa published in January 1995, DJs play from two DAT
  decks through a small mixer, cannot beat-mix, and exchange tapes **[D, S33]**; Erez Eisen still played DATs in
  1999 and ran his first live show from one **[D, S40]**.

### 3.9 Recall

- None. Toby Marks: a tune left and set up again never sounds the same, and sometimes that helps **[D, S10]**. Colin
  Bennun (OOOD): moving the studio in mid-track would lose the sound **[D, S16]**. Benji Vaughan dislikes being
  committed to the desk when he has several things in work at once **[D, S2]**.
- What was done about it, documented outside goa only: Polaroids of the desk, and finishing a track in one go because
  bringing it back was too much work (Chicane) **[D, S35]**; labelled strips laid on the desk for each tune of a
  live set (Underworld) **[D, S27]**. For a goa artist **[N]**.

## 5. Uncertain or not found

- **How "Twisted" (1995) and "The Lone Deranger" (1997) were mixed.** Where is now answered by the sleeves: all
  writing and sound work at The Hallucinogen Sound Laboratories, London, for the first, at the Hallucinogen Sound
  Labs, Dorset, for the second **[D, S54]**; Doof's 1996 album credits the same London studio **[D, S17]**. The
  Dragonfly label manager's remark that ties "Twisted" to Posford's work in the Butterfly studio with Youth
  **[D, S15]** then describes his job there, not where the album was made **[I]**. Still open: his sequencer and
  recorder of the 1990s, and whether the 1990s mixes were single passes to DAT **[N]** (the 2013 Reddit AMA, S19,
  and the video, S53, say nothing on it; the video shows the method of about 2008).
- **Butterfly's desk after 1993 [N].** The studio grew to three (Ott) or five (Youth) rooms and ran for about six
  years **[D, S13, S12]**; no later source names a console.
- **Consoles of Astral Projection, Total Eclipse, Transwave, X-Dream, Etnica / Pleiadians, Koxbox, The Infinity
  Project, Green Nuns of the Revolution, Man With No Name, Doof, Cosmosis, the Flying Rhino acts [N].** Their
  interviews, where found and read, do not discuss equipment. No magazine studio report was found for any
  continental or Israeli act.
- **The claim that the Mackie 24•8 / 32•8 was the standard goa desk** rests, beyond the three studios in section 1,
  on forum posts seen only as search-result snippets (psynews.org answered HTTP 500 all day) and on one anonymous
  post read through the Wayback Machine that names Mackie and Soundcraft Spirit as the class of desk **[D, S46,
  weak]**. Not counted as evidence.
- **Eat Static's recorders.** The gear list has a DAT machine and a DA38 8-track; the writer of the same article
  remarks on the lack of multitrack facilities. What the mixes went to, and what the DA38 was for, is not said
  **[N]**.
- **A weak claim not used above:** a reissue compiler reports second-hand that one track of the period, credited
  to Graham & Serge, exists only as two desk mixes recorded straight to DAT **[D, S47, weak]**.
- **GMS starting on PC software [N]**; the biographies read say only that a friend had bought recording equipment.

## 6. What this means for goa-console

Observations only, each set against the decision it touches in `docs/decisions_summary.md`. Nothing here is a
proposal; a design change needs its own proposal and Ruslan's sign-off.

**Inputs and returns** (decision: one identical line strip per channel, 24 of them; 8 stereo returns, two per group,
each with level, PFL and routing to MIX / 1-2 / 3-4, no EQ and no aux send, master spec §16).
- The three documented Mackie studios had 32 full channels, and one of them 56; all could double that with Mix-B
  paths of lower grade (two sends borrowed, EQ shared) **[S1, S2, S10, S20]**. goa-console has 24 full channels and
  16 return inputs, 40 signals at mixdown, and no second-grade path: every synth input gets the full strip.
- The shortage the sources name is inputs: reviewers say so of the whole class **[S21]**, and Eat Static's samplers
  alone brought ten, then twenty outputs **[S1]**. The Mackie's six stereo returns were called generous **[S20]**;
  there are eight here. That channels mattered more than returns is my reading **[I]**.
- The master spec's rule that anything needing EQ or a send takes a strip matches what one review says in passing:
  reverb returns that are to be equalised come back through channels **[S31]**. Posford's echo sent back into
  itself **[S53]** is such a case: it needs the delay's return on a path with a send **[I]**. On goa-console that
  is a strip, one per mono return, out of the 24; the eight plain returns suit effects that are only blended in.
- Fluke's Delta of 1994 was ordered as 8 stereo + 16 standard + 8 deluxe modules **[S24]**: a Delta-family console
  split by source type. goa-console's choice of one identical strip is the opposite approach on the same family.

**Aux count** (decision: 6 aux, PRE switch per pair, all six pots on every strip; aux 6 becomes the direct-out level
when DIR is latched).
- A Mackie channel reaches six buses with four knobs, and gives two away when Mix-B needs sends **[S20]**; a strip
  here has six knobs on six buses at once. Posford's one remark on sends is a doubt whether an SSL AWS900 has enough
  physical ones **[S2]**; one reviewer's view was that there are never enough **[S23]**; the Ghost and the Spirit
  Studio LC went to ten **[S31, S21]**.
- Six buses is what the three Mackie studios had available; how many they used is not documented **[I, from S20;
  N]**. Here none is shared or switched.

**PFL only, no stereo solo** (decision: PFL only, mono, pre-fader; no solo-in-place).
- The Mackie as shipped did the opposite: a stereo, post-fader, post-pan solo, with PFL only as an internal
  modification **[S20, `docs/console_survey/mackie.md`]**. So an unmodified 8•Bus played a
  soloed channel at its mix level, in its pan position **[I, from the drawings]**; that way of listening is not on
  goa-console. Whether Posford, Eat Static or Banco de Gaia left the solo as shipped is not known **[N]**.
- The one statement found on solo as a working tool is Youth's: solo buttons played to change the arrangement
  **[S3]**. Read as a solo that cuts the other channels **[I]**, it is a destructive solo-in-place, which the Mackie
  did not have either. On goa-console that move
  is made with the ON keys (and they also cut the pre-fade sends and the direct out, strip spec §11).
- No goa artist's account of solo or PFL use was found **[N]**, so the sources give nothing for or against the
  decision beyond the two points above.

**Direct outs** (decision: an impedance-balanced direct out per strip; DIR turns the aux 6 pot into its level and
takes that strip off the aux 6 bus; its source follows the aux 5-6 PRE selection; ON mutes it).
- The 8•Bus had a direct out on every channel and insert jacks usable as pre-fader direct outs **[S20]**. None of
  the goa sources mentions using them. Juno Reactor (1995) and Man With No Name recorded the stereo mix only
  **[S5, S6]**; for Eat Static and OOOD that is inferred **[I, section 3.8]**.
- The cost of that method is documented: no parts for the remixer **[S6]**, a mix that does not come back the same
  **[S10]**. Audio moved into the computer from 1996 on **[S1, S10, S42]**; that this is where direct outs find their
  use, as a multitrack record of the same live pass, is my inference **[I]**. On goa-console a strip that feeds a
  recorder this way has five aux sends left.

**Mutes and automation** (decision: ON key with the J112 soft mute, by hand; no mute automation, no mute groups;
relay mute on the outputs; an unpopulated VCA option on the strip).
- Posford's arrangement tool is the channel on / off button **[S53]**; on goa-console that is the ON key, which
  also cuts the strip's pre-fade sends and direct out (strip spec §11).
- This is the period's condition, by necessity on the Mackie until 1996 **[S20, S32]** and by choice for Youth, Ed
  Wynne and Toby Marks **[S3, S34, S10]**. The mix as a performance on faders, keys and sends is what the sources
  describe most consistently **[S5, S4, S10]**.
- The one use found for automated mutes was against the hiss of many analogue synths **[S9]**; by hand that is the
  ON key, strip by strip.
- No recall either, then or here. The only answers found are a mainstream trance producer's: finish the track
  rather than bring it back, photograph the desk **[S35, not goa]**.

**Not asked, but in the same sources.**
- Both channels are four-band with two swept mids. The Mackie adds a Q control on its high-mid and has a fixed
  75 Hz low-cut switch **[S20]**; goa-console has a sweepable high-pass instead (strip decisions EQM1, H1-H4).
- The one EQ gesture documented is Posford's sweep of a boosted, fairly low band **[S53]**. The Mackie's low-mid
  sweeps 45 Hz-3 kHz **[S20]**; goa-console's LO-MID sweeps 165 Hz-2.54 kHz and its HI-MID 598 Hz-9.93 kHz (EQM1),
  so the same gesture starts higher here.
- Two of the acts say they owned no compressors **[S7, S26]**; goa-console's four subs with inserts and the external
  bus compressor on the master insert go beyond what those studios had.
- Nothing was found on running the desk hot in goa **[N]**, so the sources say nothing about the internal 0 dBu
  level or the +18.4 dBu overload threshold.
